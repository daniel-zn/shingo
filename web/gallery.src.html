An interface style for iOS and the web: sage slabs on a striped ground, one coral diagonal, nested stadium pills and telemetry in mono. English leads, with Japanese and français woven in sparingly. Everything here is drawn in code, and the same parts ship as SwiftUI views.

[ two modes · same layering ]
darkink ground, sage slabs
lightsage ground, paper slabs

Neither mode is flat. Dark keeps the poster's ink stripes. Light moves the stripes onto the poster's own sage and lifts the slabs to paper, never white. Ink buttons, slots, the tab bar and coral stay the same in both modes, so the look survives the switch. The page follows your system setting. Use the switch in the corner to compare.

Poster study

Nested stadium pill

Three stacked capsules and a slot line. Proportions: band 18% of height, core 15%, slot 3%. Doubles as a hero, card, scrubber or progress bar. .pill · NestedPill()

Rounded X

The union of two crossed stadiums. It comes outlined, solid or faint. Use it for status, done or rest days, close and error. #g-x-line · RoundedX()

Stripe field

A soft repeating gradient with a 6 pt period. Facets are extra layers with a different angle and a clip-path. .tex .facet · StripeField (Canvas)

The cut

Every slab starts with the same 28 pt rise and a 12 pt coral edge. The steeper 45° band is kept for poster screens. .u-cut .u-slab · SGCut

Chamfer frame

A hairline outline with two cut corners. It frames fields, groups and ID cards. .chamfer · ChamferRect()

Instrument badges

The topo disc, record rings, radar fan, barcode lines, crosshair and arrows. All are generated, so they recolor with tokens. assets/svg/*

AXIOMsp c53

Type pair

Archivo Expanded Black for the wordmark, Archivo Medium with tight tracking for labels, and JetBrains Mono for right-aligned telemetry. All are OFL and bundled for iOS.

Wordmark type at 44 for display, 28 for titles and 15 inline. Nothing in between.

Besides the cut, coral marks at most one action plus small states like unread and active tab.

06Tag and aside

Japanese lives in the header tag or a seal. French lives in the subline. Each appears once at most.

Three languages

english · 日本語 · français
EN

English runs the interface

Every action, label and number. If someone has to read it to use the app, it's in English.

Protected
日本語

Japanese adds texture

Vertical captions along edges, seal stamps and outlined display kanji. Use one Japanese moment per screen at most. It is never the only label on a control.

Fr.

French is the aside

Italic footnotes, dates, taglines and pull quotes. Use it once per screen at most, usually in the quietest spot.
