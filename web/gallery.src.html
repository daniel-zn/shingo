An industrial interface system for iOS and the web. Interfaces are printed on a striped ground: hard 45° cuts, hazard bars, chevrons, barcodes and mono telemetry, with one coral signal per screen. English leads, with Japanese and français woven in sparingly.

darkink ground, bone print
lightsage ground, ink print

Every screen ships in both modes with the same layering. Dark prints bone on ink stripes. Light prints ink on the poster's sage, with paper plates that are never white. Coral is the same in both. The page follows your system setting, and the switch in the corner flips it.

Screens

iPhone 390 × 844 pt · one grammar, two tiers · placeholder brand "AXIOM"
01Two inks

Everything is print on ground: solid shapes in the print ink with text knocked out, or outlines. No third tone. Coral is the only extra ink.

02One grammar

Masthead, one hero, a divider, the body, the imprint. Each screen sits on one backdrop composition from the shape alphabet. Poster screens compose freely.

03One angle

Every diagonal rises left to right at 45°: corner cuts (top-left and bottom-right), hazard stripes, cells, bands and the stripe field. No tilts, textures or shadows.

04Three sizes

Wordmark type at 44 for display, 26 for titles and 15 inline. Labels are condensed caps and telemetry is mono.

05One signal

Coral marks one block or one primary action per screen, plus the latest or active state. Switches turn on in print, not coral.

06Tag and aside

Japanese lives in the masthead tag or a seal. French lives in the span rule. Each appears once at most.

Origins

reference 1, the sage and coral poster, rebuilt from primitives

Reference 1 gave Shingō its palette: sage, ink and coral, the striped ground, the nested forms and the right-aligned telemetry.

Reference 2, a trilingual technical print, gave it its voice: wide industrial type, hazard bars, chevrons, barcodes and the line-art head. It also brought English with Japanese and French woven in.

Shingō now leans toward the second reference. Pills became cut bars, rounded X marks became hard ones, and soft panels gave way to print. The poster stays here as the source of the color and the layering.
